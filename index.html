Know every song that played.

Wherever the music is coming from — a DJ, a bar, a party, someone else's aux — start Catchy when it starts. It listens along and names what it hears, hour after hour, and is honest about the bits it couldn't. Afterwards the whole thing is yours: a tracklist, a timeline of what played, and a card you can send your friends.

Shazam catches one song. Catchy catches everything that played.

Capturing a set, reviewing it and sharing it stay free. Always. Catchy Pro adds Insights, Apple Music export and capture with no signal.

step 1 · while the music is playing

Start it once, then tap for what matters.

Tap the disc for any song you have to have — that's the one it answers on the spot, and it works straight from the lock screen without unlocking. In between, leave the phone where it can hear and it keeps adding what it can pick up on its own. Anything it can't name is still marked, with the moment saved, so nothing disappears quietly.

step 2 · whenever you get to it

Settle the maybes in a minute.

The sure things are already in the playlist. For everything it was only half-sure about, you hear the exact seconds your phone caught and keep it or drop it — or hit Keep all and be done. Moments it couldn't name are tucked into Unknown at the bottom, honestly labelled, with the clip kept in case you want to hunt the ID later.

step 3 · the set, kept

Everything that played, in order.

Each session becomes a set: the tracklist in order, a timeline you can drag a finger across to see what was playing when, and the ⋯ menu carrying the verbs as words — including send a set straight to your own Apple Music library, once the set is reviewed. That one is part of Catchy Pro. Every song you keep lands in your library on its own, with the set it came from still attached. The ★ is for the ones that were the night.

step 4 · for the group chat

Proof you were there.

Every set renders a card — the venue, the length, the timeline of what played, your rating, and how many moments stayed a mystery. Send the card, or send the link — the link opens the whole set for anyone, no app and no account. The card even prints the address, so a screenshot is still a doorway. Both of these are the app as it ships. The Friends tab is where people arrive and where their sets land; the bell is what they did with yours.

  • Add a friend by @handle, by code, or by link. Search a username and tap Ask to connect, trade codes in person, or send a link — whoever opens it can ask from there. With a table of people, start a room: one code for everyone, and it closes itself in fifteen minutes.

Catchy Pro · the paid tier

Small, and it says so.

The app is free and stays free. Catchy Pro is one subscription on top of it — Insights, Apple Music export, and capture with no signal — from the App Store, starting with a free trial.

  • What's free is free for good. Capturing a set, reviewing it and sharing it stay free. Always. So are your library, your stats, your friends, the cards and the pages you share — and recognition is never behind the tier.
  • ✦ Insights, on any song you caught. Pro Insights is the story behind the set — the artist, the record, and where to go next, on any song you caught. Three are free every month without Pro; the counter only shows up once you've spent the first one.
  • Apple Music export. Pro Send a set straight to your own Apple Music library. Permission is asked once, and it needs an Apple Music subscription on the phone.
  • Capture with no signal. Pro In a basement with no service, the stretches it couldn't identify wait on your phone and get their names the moment you're back online.
  • It's a subscription, bought from the App Store. It starts with a free trial, it renews until you cancel, and you manage or cancel it in your Apple account settings — the price is whatever the App Store shows you there.

It listens. Here is exactly what it keeps.

An app that holds a microphone open for hours owes you a straight answer about where the sound goes.

  • The sound itself never leaves. Your phone turns what it hears into a fingerprint on the device and sends only that — never the audio — to Apple's Shazam service to be matched. The audio it fingerprinted is dropped on the spot.
  • Audio is kept only where you'll be asked to judge it — one short clip for each song it was unsure about, and each moment it couldn't name. They stay on your phone, delete themselves when you finish reviewing the set (or after 30 days if you never do), and leave the device only if you deliberately share one. The one exception is a clip you tap Keep clip on: that one is yours until you delete it.
  • When a match can't be made right then — no signal, or the service not answering — that stretch of audio waits on the phone until it can be identified, and is deleted the moment it is. That's the price of not losing it.
  • An account is real, and it's for friends. Capturing, reviewing and your library need no sign-up and no password, and opening a set someone shares with you needs nothing at all. Signing in (Apple or Google) is what backs your friend code, your friends list and your share links, so they survive a new phone — and adding a friend is the one thing that asks for it. Signing in picks your @username too — the server holds it, it's how friends find you, and you can change it later (once every 30 days) — along with a display name if you add one, and a profile carrying your counters, the disc you chose and your Instagram handle if you added one, all of which you can switch off. Once you have friends, a finished set goes to them unless you switch that off — Catchy asks the first time it happens, and any set can be unshared. Your audio never leaves either way.
  • No analytics SDK, no tracking, no trail of where you go. The only time Catchy uses your location is when you tap Pin the venue, once per set, to save where the set was. Your exact coordinates never leave your phone. What travels is the venue's name and an approximate location — a map cell about 150 m across — and only where the set itself goes: with a set you share, or to a friend you let join you. The cell is there so that friends' phones at the same place can be joined into one shared set. Nothing else about where you are ever leaves your phone. Our server does the connecting — friend links, invitations, the pages you share — and never sees your audio, or the name of a set you didn't share (a private set sends only the diagnostics and usage data below, as catalog identifiers, counts and times — and an Insights question when you ask one). Open a song in Spotify and a random install ID goes along with that one request and nothing else; it's tied to nothing about you and resets if you reinstall.
  • Diagnostics and usage data, each with its own switch. After a set ends, again after it is reviewed, and again if its numbers change, the app sends capture diagnostics: counters about the capture and each hour of it, one row per song with what you did with it in review, and every recognition window — catalog identifiers and numbers, plus your device model and iOS version. A small start record leaves when the microphone starts, and a device summary once a day. While you use it, the app sends usage data: what you do in it, as counts and times, with the app build and your device model and iOS version. Never audio, never a recording, never song titles, set names or anything you typed. Both exist to fix problems and make the app better. Switching either off (Share usage data is the second) deletes what it sent; deleting a set deletes that set's rows.
  • Ask about an artist, and that one question leaves the phone. Open a song's card and Catchy starts the ✦ Insights lookup so the answer is ready if you tap: the song's title and artist go to our server, and the artist's name to MusicBrainz, the public music encyclopedia, to make sure it's the right one. Any set, shared or private — it's you asking about an artist, not the set reporting itself, and nothing else about the set goes along.
